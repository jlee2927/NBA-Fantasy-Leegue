The draft assistant

The board above is a fixed ranking. The assistant is the other half: you enter your first pick, it proposes the punt builds that suit that player, and from then on it re-ranks everyone still available around the build you chose and the roster you are building. It runs either as a mock draft against simulated managers, or alongside a real draft happening elsewhere with picks typed in as they go.

That part has to run on a server rather than as a page, so it is not here yet. The rankings below are the same ones it uses.

What the numbers mean

Every category is scored as standard deviations from the draft-pool average, so each one counts equally. A player's Value is the mean of those nine scores. That is why one extra steal per game is worth roughly eighteen extra points per game: almost every draftable player gets about one steal, so the player getting two is genuinely rare, while one more point barely separates anybody.

Punting a category means dropping it from that average entirely. A manager who gives up on turnovers stops paying for the turnovers a high-usage guard commits, which is why those guards climb the board under that build.

Where the projections come from

  • Marcel projections over 25 seasons of play-by-play data, weighting the last three seasons 5–4–3 and regressing toward league average by an amount fitted per category.
  • Rookies have no NBA seasons, so their college production is translated using factors fitted on every player since 2015 who did both, with playing time predicted from draft slot. They are labelled and carry a confidence.
  • Nothing is hand-tuned. Every constant is fitted from prior seasons and checked against seasons it was not fitted on.

How it was checked

Each season is projected using only parameters fitted on earlier seasons, and one season is held back from every stage of tuning.

  • Walk-forward backtest — 10% better than carrying last season forward, and the held-out season agrees.
  • Rookie translation backtest — nine of eleven categories beat guessing the class average. Steals do not.
  • Source parity check — three independent data sources compared before choosing one.

Projections are precomputed and refreshed between seasons, not live during a draft. Built with public data from sportsdataverse. Not affiliated with the NBA.
